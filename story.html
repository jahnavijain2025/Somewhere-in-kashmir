WHY KASHMIR?

A journey that left me wanting more.

This summer, I had the chance to visit Kashmir, and what began as a few days away soon became an experience I wished could have lasted a little longer.

I found myself surrounded by landscapes that hardly seemed real — peaceful lakes reflecting the mountains, winding roads disappearing into the hills, endless stretches of green, and views that made you want to stop for just one more moment.

But Kashmir was more than its beautiful scenery. It was the feeling of being there: the calm, the quiet, the sense of wonder, and the feeling that every turn had something new waiting to be discovered.

Four or five days felt far too short. Just when I thought I had seen so much, I realised how much more there was left to explore. Valleys I hadn't visited, roads I hadn't travelled, and experiences I hadn't had the time to make part of my own story.

And that's when a saying began to make perfect sense:

"Once you enter Kashmir, you just can't leave."

Of course, every journey eventually brings you home. But sometimes, a place stays with you long after you've left it. A part of your heart remains among its mountains, its valleys, and the moments you wish you could experience all over again.

I left Kashmir with beautiful memories, a camera full of moments, and one thought that kept coming back: there is still so much more to see.

That feeling became the inspiration behind Somewhere in Kashmir. I wanted to create a guide that helps travellers look beyond the usual plans, discover places they might otherwise miss, and make every day of their journey count.

THE IDEA BEHIND THIS PROJECT

Less time wondering.
More time discovering.

Not every traveller has a week to spare. Sometimes, three or four days are all you have — and they deserve to be unforgettable, too.

Somewhere in Kashmir brings destinations, lesser-known escapes, and itinerary planning together in one place, helping you spend less time figuring things out and more time experiencing the valley.

Because Kashmir has a way of making you want to return. And perhaps the best journeys are the ones that leave you with a reason to come back.
